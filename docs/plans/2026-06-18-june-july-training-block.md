## Risk Map

### 1. Frequency jump risk

Going from roughly `3x/week` to `5x/week` can work, but only if the extra two sessions are low-cost.

Failure mode:
- too many medium-hard days
- cardio fatigue spills into lifting motivation
- recovery gets noisy and attendance drops

Control:
- July uses `3 main sessions + 2 low-cost sessions`
- only `3` sessions per week should feel like real training stress
- the other `2` are there to build consistency, conditioning base, and calorie expenditure

### 2. Conditioning is ahead of neither motivation nor recovery

The logs say the finisher is what gets hard first.

Failure mode:
- finishers become dread-inducing
- the session ends feeling like a cardio failure even when the lifting went well

Control:
- keep finishers repeatable
- prefer zone 2, short intervals, incline walk, recumbent bike, or sled work over heroic efforts
- do not progress duration and intensity at the same time

### 3. False caution on the main lifts

You are probably more tolerant than your injury history makes you feel.

Failure mode:
- keeping the main strength work too easy for too long
- losing progression signal
- mistaking underloading for safety

Control:
- progress anchor lifts modestly when knee response stays calm
- change one thing at a time: load, set count, or exercise variation

### 4. Repetition fatigue

You do not currently need chaos, but too much sameness will eventually flatten motivation.

Failure mode:
- sessions feel stale
- you start skipping because everything feels identical

Control:
- keep `2 to 4` anchors stable
- rotate accessories, conditioning mode, and trunk work

### 5. Gaps reset momentum

This is still the highest leverage operational risk.

Failure mode:
- several missed days turn progression into another re-entry week

Control:
- when in doubt, do the lighter session instead of skipping the day
- protect streaks more than perfect programming

## Operating Rules

- Pain rule: if knee pain rises above `3/10`, or swelling appears afterward, next lower-body session gets lighter and simpler.
- Progression rule: only one of these should go up in a given session: load, set count, or conditioning density.
- July intensity rule: `5` training days does not mean `5` hard days.
- Recovery rule: at least `2` off or low-demand evenings per week should stay intact.
- Variety rule: anchors stay, accessories rotate.

## Session Types

### A. Lower + trunk strength

- Leg press or similar controlled knee-dominant pattern
- Hip thrust or posterior-chain main lift
- Hamstring accessory
- Trunk anti-rotation or dead bug variation
- optional short easy finisher

### B. Upper + posterior strength

- Dumbbell bench press
- Row or pulldown
- Hip thrust or hinge-lite posterior-chain slot
- trunk work
- optional easy zone 2 close

### C. Full-body progression day

- one knee-dominant movement
- one upper push
- one upper pull
- one posterior-chain movement
- one trunk movement
- very small finisher dose only

### D. Low-cost conditioning / recovery day

- `20 to 35 min` zone 2 bike, incline walk, or elliptical
- plus `10 to 15 min` mobility, trunk, calves, tibialis, or light unilateral control
- should finish feeling better, not smashed

### E. Conditioning + accessory day

- short repeatable intervals or steady aerobic work
- plus optional arms, shoulders, calves, adductors, abductors, or trunk
- this is not a secret hard leg day

## What Should Progress

- Main lift loads: small bumps when the prior exposure felt clearly manageable
- Conditioning: first make it more finishable, then make it longer, then make it harder
- Session count: July adds days, not five hard efforts

## What Should Not Progress Yet

- hard finisher density
- high-impact work
- aggressive twisting or reactive knee work
- large lower-body volume jumps

## If You Miss A Day

- miss a low-cost day: do not reshuffle the whole week
- miss a main day: slide it once, then drop the least important conditioning day
- miss two sessions in a row: next session becomes a re-entry version, not a pride session

## Success Markers By July 31

- you are actually training `5x/week` in July without feeling wrecked
- knee stays at `0 to 2/10` pain and no swelling trend appears
- bench and hip thrust are clearly above current June baselines
- finishers feel more routine and less like the part that breaks the session
- the streak feels normal enough that August does not need another reset
